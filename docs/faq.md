# Frequently Asked Questions

## Team Operation

**How big should a team be?** Golazo generally works for teams of 4 to 10 engineers. For a first
trial, 6 to 8 is the easiest size to start with: there are enough people to pair and review without
losing track of what others are doing. Larger groups can use multiple boards with explicit
coordination between leads or coaches.

**Can a single engineer use Golazo practices?** Yes. Keep tickets small, write short design notes,
and limit what you work on at once. If someone else can review a change, ask them to take a look.

**What if a ticket stalls?** Bring it up at the next standup. If it's still stuck, find out what
it's waiting for: another team's work, a review, a decision about scope, or someone to validate it.

## Adoption and Transition

**How long is the trial?** Usually about 90 days. That gives the team several planning cycles to see
whether the new practices cut down on rework.

**Do we customize Golazo early?** Usually no. If a practice stops the team from doing its work,
write down why before changing it. Otherwise, try the practices together before deciding what to
drop.

**What if leadership pressures for more simultaneous work?** Show what's already in progress and
which tickets will likely take longer if you start more.

## Roles and Ownership

**What happens if someone picks a ticket beyond their current expertise?** Pair early and ask for
review before too much is built. It may take longer this time, but next time more than one person
will know the area.

**How do senior engineers show growth?** Shape work, review designs, mentor teammates, improve the
system, and help teams work together. Use examples from design docs and reviews to show that work in
career conversations.

## Quality and Design Docs

**Aren't design docs overhead for tiny changes?** For a small change, a few bullets may be enough.
Write down the assumptions a reviewer needs to check.

**What if reviewers disagree?** Capture the options and tradeoffs in the design doc, then resolve
the decision in a focused follow-up conversation if needed. After that, update the document so the
outcome is visible to later readers.

**Why require two design signoffs and two pull request approvals?** A second reviewer may spot
something the first missed, and more people learn how the change works. Small tickets make those
reviews easier to do.

## Metrics and Success

**How do we know it’s working?** Look for shorter cycle times, fewer incidents, and fewer urgent
tickets crowding out planned work. Check whether more people can pick up work outside their usual
areas without chasing down someone for context.

**What if metrics improve but morale drops?** Ask what's making work harder. For example, tickets
may finish sooner because people are squeezing in reviews after hours.

**Is velocity or story point tracking required?** No. Look at how many tickets finish, how long they
take, how long they wait for review, and whether defects show up after release.

## Miscellaneous

**Can we use sprints?** Yes. The simpler default is to keep work moving between planning meetings
without committing to a fixed batch each time.

**How do we manage external stakeholder asks?** Put them on the board, define acceptance criteria,
and prioritize them with the rest of the work. Side channels create invisible work and make the
system harder to trust.

## Still Have Questions?

Open a GitHub issue or discussion with enough context to explain what you are trying to do and where
the current guidance is unclear.
