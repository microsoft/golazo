# Golazo

## Summary

Golazo is a way for small and midsize engineering teams to organize their work. It helps when pull
requests turn into design debates, engineers juggle too many tickets, or only one person knows why a
decision was made.

Keep tickets small, agree on an approach before coding, and help finish work before starting more.
Check with the customer or stakeholder that the change did what they needed.

## Who It’s For

- Teams that want to see what's in progress and who's handling it
- Teams that work in person, in a hybrid setup, or across time zones
- Product or platform teams that can share enough context to work outside their usual areas
- Teams that spend too much time revisiting decisions in review or handling urgent fixes
- Organizations that want more predictable delivery without adding a lot of meetings or paperwork

Golazo generally works for teams of 4 to 10 engineers, though other team sizes can still use parts
of it. If you're new to Golazo, 6 to 8 engineers is the easiest size to start with.

## What Makes It Different

- Write a short design doc and get peer signoff before coding
- Set explicit limits on work in progress
- Update the board as work happens so others can see where to help
- Keep tickets small enough to change priorities without abandoning weeks of work
- Treat a ticket waiting for review as work the team can help finish
- Let any engineer pull a ticket from Ready
- Check that the change delivered what the customer needed before calling it done

## Inspiration

Golazo builds on ideas found in several books:

- "Implementing Lean Software Development: From Concept to Cash" by Mary and Tom Poppendieck
- "The Principles of Product Development Flow: Second Generation Lean Product Development" by Donald
  G. Reinertsen
- "Kanban: Successful Evolutionary Change for Your Technology Business" by David J. Anderson
- "The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to Create Radically
  Successful Businesses" by Eric Ries

Golazo is one way to put these ideas into practice, not a formal standard.
