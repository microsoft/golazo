# Principles

## Summary

Finish small pieces of work, check that they helped, and make it easy for teammates to pick up where
someone left off. These principles explain why Golazo organizes work that way.

## Core Principles

### Deliver Quickly

Deliver changes in pieces that people can try on their own. If a change misses the mark, you'll find
out while there's still time to adjust the rest.

### Eliminate Waste

Look for work that takes time without helping customers or making future work easier: waiting for a
review, repeating a manual step, or reopening a debate because nobody wrote down the assumptions.

### Defer Commitment

Prepare early, but leave options open until you have enough information to choose. A backlog item
can describe the problem without deciding what to build.

### Build Quality In

Make design, review, testing, and monitoring part of finishing the ticket. If they're left until
after release, the next person on call may be the first to find out what was missed.

### Create Knowledge

Write down why you chose an approach so the next person can understand it without tracking you down.
Review comments and rotating work across the team help people build that knowledge too.

### Respect People

Give people time to focus. If a teammate can only keep up by switching between emergencies all day,
reduce the amount of work in progress or help finish some of it.

### Succeed and Fail Together

The team owns the outcome. When a ticket goes well, ask what helped. When it goes badly, ask what
made the failure likely and what the team can change.

### Optimize the Whole

An engineer can pick up something new while a teammate waits for a review. Reviewing the
near-finished ticket first may get more work to Done.

## Applying the Principles

- Deliver Quickly: keep tickets under the team SLA and prefer slices that a stakeholder can validate
  independently.
- Eliminate Waste: visualize blockers, note backward movement, and inspect recurring sources of
  delay in retrospectives.
- Defer Commitment: capture ideas in the backlog without forcing detailed design before the work is
  close to being started.
- Build Quality In: define tests, monitoring, and documentation expectations before implementation
  begins.
- Create Knowledge: use design docs and review expectations to spread context instead of preserving
  it in private conversations.
- Respect People: honor WIP limits so people can focus and have time to help when priorities change.
- Succeed and Fail Together: treat incidents, missed estimates, and rework as opportunities to
  improve the system rather than assign personal blame.
- Optimize the Whole: when you have time, review a change or unblock a ticket before pulling new
  work.
